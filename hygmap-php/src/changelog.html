Change log

  • 2026-7-31

    • Fixed the note explaining why a star cannot be mapped: it was black text on a dark brown background, near enough to invisible. It is now a light panel that you can actually read.
    • Fixed stars with no known distance being drawn at the Sun. About 25,000 stars in the catalog have never had their distance measured, and the map was treating "unknown" as "zero" — so they were plotted right on top of Sol, and clicking one recentred the whole map there.
    • You can still find those stars by searching, and the info panel now tells you what is actually known about them: their position on the sky and their brightness, with the distance shown as unknown and a note explaining that they cannot be placed on the map. Previously searching for one took you to a view of the Sun with that star's name on it.
    • Fixed around 460 stars showing up as the wrong colour in the 3D app. Carbon stars and a few other cool, deep-red types were being drawn plain white there while the 2D map drew them red or orange, and subdwarfs were mis-read so they lost their colour entirely. Both views now agree.

  • 2026-7-30

    • Fixed several thousand stars appearing on the map twice. Two of the catalogs the database is built from were each adding the same nearby star as if it were new, so one physical star could show up as two markers and two search results. About 2,600 stars were affected.
    • Note that a few hundred close double stars still show as two entries, and that is correct: they really are two stars, close enough together that some catalogs record only one. Graffias and Graffias B, for example, are a genuine pair.
    • Fixed one star in Cepheus that was being shown at the wrong distance. It had been given the identity of a star in Leo, 95 degrees away, and had inherited that star's distance and motion along with it. Its distance is now blank rather than wrong, since no reliable measurement of it exists.
    • Zoomed-out views draw much faster. Asking for a wide field of stars used to make the database sort through most of the catalog and spill to disk; it now reads them in order and stops as soon as it has enough. The widest views went from around 1.6 seconds to a fifth of a second, and the middle range improved even more.
    • Fixed the same view sometimes showing a slightly different set of stars each time you loaded it. Huge numbers of stars share exactly the same brightness value, and when the list was cut off at a limit it was arbitrary which of those equally-bright stars made it in. The order is now settled, so the same view always gives you the same stars.
    • You can now search for a star by its fictional name. With Star Trek switched on in the settings, searching "Vulcan" takes you straight to Keid, the real star it is meant to be -- previously it found nothing at all, and the only way to reach a fictional star was the drop-down list. Partial names work too, so "vulc" is enough.
    • Fictional names only match the universe you have selected. With Babylon 5 switched on, "Vulcan" will not find anything, and with the fiction layer off it finds nothing either. This keeps a star from being found under a name the map is not showing for it.
    • Looking a star up by its catalog number now also knows its fictional name: with Star Trek on, searching "HD 26965" arrives at a star labelled "Vulcan", the same as the map calls it.
    • Fixed 17 nearby stars being drawn thousands of parsecs away. Each had had its distance corrected from a newer catalog, but the position on the map was still the one worked out from the old, wrong distance. GJ 125 is 17 parsecs from the Sun and was being plotted at 97,000. All 17 are now where they belong, and the map is checked against this before it is published.
    • Fixed star names disagreeing with themselves on a single page. With a fiction naming layer switched on, the info panel and the star table showed a star's catalog name while the map itself showed its fictional name -- 37 Cet was labelled "37 Cet (Alpha)", "37 Cet" and "Alpha" in three places at once. The fictional name now comes first everywhere, with the catalog name kept alongside it in the info panel.
    • Searching by constellation now finds far more stars. Around 281,000 stars from the newer catalogs had no constellation recorded, so a search for "Cyg" or "Ori" could never return them; their constellations have been computed from their positions.
    • The 3D app can now jump straight to a set of coordinates: click the crosshair in the toolbar and type a galactic X, Y and Z. It accepts whichever unit you have selected, so you can copy the numbers off a star's info panel and go back to them later.

  • 2026-7-29

    • Star names are now identical between this map and the 3D app. Where a star has both a Gliese and a Hipparcos number, both now show the Gliese designation -- the nearby-star catalog is the more useful label for a map of the solar neighbourhood.
    • Fixed stars whose only catalog entry was a Yale Bright Star (HR) number showing no label at all.
    • Fixed stars with a Bayer letter but no constellation showing a stray label such as "Alp " instead of a catalog number.
    • The map tooltip now says "Abs Mag" rather than "Mag", which was ambiguous -- the value shown is absolute magnitude.
    • Star search is much faster, and searching for "Alp Cen" now works as well as "Alpha Cen".
